## BENEFICIARY NOMINATION GUIDELINES

VitaLife Insurance Corporation | 2025 Edition

## 1. WHY NOMINATION MATTERS

## Importance of a Valid Nomination

Nomination is one of the most critical steps in life insurance. A valid, up-to-date nomination ensures that your death benefit reaches your intended beneficiary quickly, without legal complications or delays. Without a valid nomination, the claim proceeds may be subject to probate proceedings, delays, or distribution disputes among legal heirs - causing significant hardship to your family at an already difficult time.

VitaLife strongly recommends reviewing your nomination at every major life event: marriage, birth of a child, divorce, or death of a nominee.

## 2. TYPES OF NOMINEES

## 2.1 Beneficial Nominee (Section 39, Insurance Act 1938)

A BENEFICIAL NOMINEE has an absolute, irrevocable right to the policy proceeds upon the death of the life assured. Creditors, legal heirs, or courts cannot attach or redirect the claim payment.

WHO QUALIFIES AS A BENEFICIAL NOMINEE: Only the following relationships qualify: - Spouse of the life assured Children of the life assured (biological or legally adopted) - Parents of the life assured - Any combination of the above

KEY  RULE:  If  you  name  your  spouse,  child,  or  parent  as  the  nominee,  they  automatically  become  a  Beneficial Nominee. You do NOT need to explicitly mark them as 'beneficial' - the relationship itself confers this status.

IMPACT ON POLICYHOLDER RIGHTS: Once a beneficial nominee is named, the policyholder CANNOT surrender, assign, or take a loan against the policy WITHOUT the written consent of the beneficial nominee (if major) or appointee (if minor). This is a significant constraint - consider this before naming a beneficial nominee if you need flexibility.

## 2.2 Other (Non-Beneficial) Nominees

Any  person  OTHER  than  spouse,  child,  or  parent  named  as  nominee  (e.g.,  sibling,  friend,  business  partner)  is  a non-beneficial nominee. They receive the death benefit as a TRUSTEE for the legal heirs of the life assured. In other words, they do not personally own the proceeds - they hold the money on behalf of and must distribute it to the legal heirs.

RECOMMENDATION:  If  you  wish  to  leave  proceeds  to  a  sibling  or  other  relative,  it  is  better  to  name  them  as beneficiary in your Will rather than as a non-beneficial nominee under the policy, to avoid disputes.

## 3. NOMINATION RULES

## 3.1 Basic Rules

- The nominee must be named at the time of policy inception or can be added/changed at any time during the policy term.  -  A  policyholder  can  name  multiple  nominees  and  specify  the  percentage  share  of  the  death  benefit  each nominee will  receive.  -  The  sum  of  all  percentage  shares  must  equal  exactly  100%.  -  If  a  nominee's  share  is  not specified,  equal  shares  are  assumed  among  all  nominees.  -  A  nominee  does  not  need  to  be  a  blood  relative  (but non-relatives will be non-beneficial nominees). - A nominee cannot be a minor organisation, company, or trust - only natural persons.

## 3.2 Nominating a Minor Nominee

If  the nominee is a minor (under 18 years of age), an APPOINTEE must also be named. The appointee is an adult (typically the other parent or a guardian) who will receive and manage the claim proceeds on behalf of the minor until the minor reaches the age of majority (18 years).

REQUIRED INFORMATION FOR MINOR NOMINEE: - Full name of minor nominee - Date of birth of minor nominee Relationship to life assured - Full name, date of birth, and relationship of appointee - Appointee's contact details When the minor nominee turns 18, the appointee's role automatically ceases and the now-adult nominee becomes the direct beneficiary. VitaLife should be notified when the minor turns 18 to update records.

## 3.3 Share Allocation Rules

Example 1 - VALID: Spouse 60%, Child 1 (daughter) 20%, Child 2 (son) 20% = 100% 3 Example  2  -  VALID: Parents equally: Father 50%, Mother 50% = 100% 3 Example 3 - INVALID: Wife 60%, Son 60% = 120% 7 (exceeds 100%) Example 4 - INVALID: Wife 40%, Son 40%, Daughter 10% = 90% 7 (does not total 100%)

If invalid shares are submitted, VitaLife will contact the policyholder to correct before processing. Policies issued with unresolved nomination errors may have claims delayed.

## 3.4 Changing a Nomination

Nomination  can  be  changed  at  any  time  during  the  policy  term  by  submitting  Form  NOM-02  (Nomination  Change Request) to any VitaLife branch or online through the policyholder portal.

IMPORTANT: The change of nomination takes effect only from the date VitaLife endorses the change on the policy. A nomination change form submitted but not yet endorsed does NOT override the existing nomination.

EXCEPTION - BENEFICIAL NOMINEE: If you have named a beneficial nominee (spouse, child, parent), you cannot change the nomination without the WRITTEN CONSENT of the current beneficial nominee (or their guardians if minor). This protects beneficial nominees from being unilaterally removed.

## 4. NOMINEE PREDECEASES THE LIFE ASSURED

## What Happens If the Nominee Dies First

If a nominee dies before the life assured and no substitute nominee has been named: The death benefit is payable to the legal heirs of the life assured as per succession laws (not as per the nominee's legal heirs). A succession certificate or probate may be required, causing delays.

BEST PRACTICE: Always name substitute nominees or update your nomination if your primary nominee passes away. You can name a second or third nominee as a backup by specifying:  'If  [Primary  Nominee]  predeceases  me,  the benefit shall pass to [Substitute Nominee].'
